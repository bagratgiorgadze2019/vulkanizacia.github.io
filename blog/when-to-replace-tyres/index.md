Old or worn tyres are one of the most common reasons a car slides on wet roads and the braking distance grows. Here is what to check.

## Tread depth

In many countries the legal minimum is 1.6 mm, but for safety it is better to replace summer tyres at 3 mm and winter tyres at 4 mm. The tread grooves have wear indicators — small raised bars. When the tread is worn down to them, the tyre must be replaced.

## Tyre age — the DOT code

The four-digit code on the sidewall shows the week and year of manufacture: for example, 2321 means week 23 of 2021. Rubber ages over time, so inspect tyres older than 6 years carefully and replace tyres older than 10 years even if the tread is still deep.

## Signs a tyre needs replacing

- Cracks on the sidewall or tread
- A bulge on the sidewall — driving on it is dangerous
- Uneven wear — may point to a pressure or suspension problem
- Vibration that stays even after balancing
- Frequent punctures on a heavily worn tyre

## Frequently asked questions

### How many years does a tyre last?

Usually 4–6 years, depending on mileage and care. After 6 years inspect the tyre every year, and after 10 years replace it.

### Can I replace just one tyre?

It is better to replace tyres in pairs — both on the same axle — so the car handles predictably.

### Should new tyres go on the front or the rear?

Most manufacturers recommend fitting new tyres on the rear axle — it reduces the risk of skidding on wet roads.
