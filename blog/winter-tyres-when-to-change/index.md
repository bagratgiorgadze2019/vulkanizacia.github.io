Winter tyres are not only for snow. In cold weather the rubber of a summer tyre hardens and grip gets worse — even on dry asphalt.

## The 7°C rule

When the average daily temperature stays below about 7°C, a winter tyre grips the road better than a summer one. In Tbilisi this usually happens from mid-November, and the switch back to summer tyres in late March or April, when the temperature stays above 7°C.

## How winter tyres differ

- Softer rubber that stays flexible in the cold
- Deeper tread and many fine sipes for grip on snow and ice
- Better braking on wet and cold roads
- M+S marking or the mountain-snowflake symbol (3PMSF)

## Mountain roads and winter trips

If you are heading to Gudauri, Bakuriani or other mountain areas in winter, fit winter tyres in advance and carry snow chains — during snowfall, mountain passes may be open only to cars with winter tyres or chains.

## What to check on winter tyres

- Tread depth — at least 4 mm is recommended for winter tyres
- Age — rubber older than 6 years hardens even if the tread is deep
- Cracks and bulges on the sidewall
- Pressure — it drops in the cold, so check it more often

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I fit only two winter tyres?

Not recommended — winter tyres should be on all four wheels so the car stays stable when braking and cornering.

### Will all-season tyres cope with winter?

For Tbilisi's mild winter, good all-season tyres are often enough, but on snow, ice and mountain roads winter tyres are much better.

### How long do winter tyres last?

Usually 4–5 seasons, depending on mileage and storage. After 6 years the rubber hardens and they are best replaced.
