Brake pads are one of the most important safety parts of a car. If they are not replaced in time, the braking distance gets longer and the brake discs get damaged too. Here is what brake pads are and 6 signs it's time to replace them.

## What are brake pads

A brake pad is a metal backing plate with a layer of friction material. When you brake, the caliper presses the pads against the brake disc and the friction stops the car. Every time you brake, the friction material wears down a little.

New vs worn brake pad — replace the pad when the friction material is down to about 3 mm.

## 6 signs your brake pads need replacing

- **Squeaking when braking** — many pads have a metal wear indicator that squeals when the pad is worn
- **Grinding** — the friction material is almost gone and metal touches the disc; replace immediately
- **Warning light on the dashboard** — on cars with a pad wear sensor
- **Longer braking distance**, or you have to press the pedal harder
- **Vibration** in the pedal or steering wheel when braking — often a disc problem
- **The car pulls to one side** when braking

## How often should brake pads be replaced

There is no exact mileage — it depends on driving style, roads and pad quality. In city traffic with frequent braking, pads wear faster. It is a good idea to check the pads roughly every 10,000 km. A new pad has about 10–12 mm of friction material; when it is down to about 3 mm, it's time to replace it.

## Front or rear brake pads

Front pads usually wear faster, because most of the braking load goes to the front axle. Pads are always replaced in pairs — on both wheels of the same axle. A disc is replaced if it is badly worn, deeply scored or causes vibration.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are brake pads?

A metal plate with a friction layer that the caliper presses against the brake disc to stop the car.

### How many kilometres do brake pads last?

There is no exact figure — it depends on driving style and roads. Check them roughly every 10,000 km and replace them when the friction material is down to about 3 mm.

### Why do my brakes squeak?

Often the wear indicator is telling you the pads are worn. New or dirty pads can also squeak — it's best to have them checked.
