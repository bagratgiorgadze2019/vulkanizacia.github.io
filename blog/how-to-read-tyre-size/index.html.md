The numbers and letters on the tyre sidewall tell you everything you need to choose a new tyre. Let's go through an example: 205/55 R16 91V.

Tyre markings explained on 205/55 R16 91V — what every number and letter means.

## Decoding the markings

- **205** — tyre width in millimetres
- **55** — profile height as a percentage of the width (55% of 205 mm)
- **R** — radial construction (almost all modern tyres)
- **16** — rim diameter in inches
- **91** — load index (91 = up to 615 kg per tyre)
- **V** — speed index (V = up to 240 km/h)

## Where to find your car's tyre size

Approved sizes are listed in the car manual and often on the sticker in the driver's door frame. The simplest way is to read the size printed on your current tyre.

## Other important markings

- **DOT** and a four-digit code — week and year of manufacture
- **M+S** or the mountain-snowflake symbol — winter or all-season tyre
- **XL** (Extra Load) — reinforced tyre for higher loads
- **Run Flat** (RSC, ROF, ZP) — can be driven a limited distance after a puncture
- **TWI** — location of the wear indicator

## Can I fit a different size?

The rim diameter must match, and the width and profile must be among the options approved by the car manufacturer. The load and speed index must not be lower than the original. If in doubt, call us — we will help you choose.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does R16 mean?

R stands for radial construction and 16 for the rim diameter in inches — the tyre fits a 16-inch rim.

### How do I find a tyre's production date?

On the sidewall, after the DOT marking, find the four-digit code: the first two digits are the week, the last two the year. For example, 0823 means week 8 of 2023.

### Can I have different tyre sizes front and rear?

Only if the car manufacturer specifies it (on some sports models). Otherwise all four tyres should be the same size.
