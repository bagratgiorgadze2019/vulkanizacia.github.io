The right pressure makes tyres last longer, saves fuel and improves braking. It is also the check people forget most often.

## Where to find the right pressure

The recommended pressure is on the sticker in the driver's door frame, inside the fuel filler flap or in the car manual. For most passenger cars it is about 2.0–2.5 bar (29–36 PSI), but always follow your car's own values — front and rear wheels, and a fully loaded car, often have different values.

## How to check it correctly

- Check cold tyres — before driving or after the car has stood for a few hours
- Check once a month and before long trips
- Don't forget the spare wheel
- Pressure drops in the cold — check more often in winter

## Dangers of low pressure

- The tyre wears quickly on the edges
- Fuel consumption goes up
- Handling and braking get worse
- Higher risk of overheating and tyre damage

## Dangers of high pressure

- The tyre wears in the centre
- Less grip, especially on wet roads
- A harsher ride

## Frequently asked questions

### What should my car's tyre pressure be?

For most passenger cars 2.0–2.5 bar (29–36 PSI). The exact value is on the driver's door frame or the fuel filler flap.

### Should I add pressure in winter?

Pressure drops in the cold, so check it more often in winter and top it up to the manufacturer's recommended value.

### Why does my tyre lose pressure?

Most often because of a puncture, a damaged rim edge, a leaking valve or natural loss over time.
